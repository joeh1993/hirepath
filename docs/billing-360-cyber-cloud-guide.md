## 1. Know the market cold

You cannot bill in a market you can't speak the language of. Cyber and cloud candidates and hiring managers will dismiss a recruiter who fakes it within two sentences. Invest the first fortnight here and keep topping it up forever.

### Why the demand exists (your BD talking points)

These are the macro drivers creating roles. Reference them in BD to sound credible and to time outreach:

- **The 2023–2030 Australian Cyber Security Strategy** — government-driven uplift, funding, and a stated ambition to make Australia a world leader by 2030.
- **SOCI Act (Security of Critical Infrastructure)** — mandatory risk-management obligations for critical-infrastructure operators (energy, water, telco, health, finance, data, food). Creates GRC, risk, and security-engineering demand.
- **APRA CPS 234 (and CPS 230 operational resilience)** — binds regulated financial entities (banks, insurers, super funds) to information-security standards. Banks and insurers are perennial buyers.
- **Essential Eight (ACSC)** — the maturity-model uplift mandate, especially across government. "Essential Eight uplift" programs are a constant source of contract GRC and engineering work.
- **Privacy Act reform** — tightening obligations post the major breaches.
- **Landmark breaches** (Optus, Medibank, Latitude, and the steady drumbeat since) — these put cyber on the board agenda and unlock budget. A breach in the news = a hiring spike weeks later.
- **Cloud migration & consolidation** — every large org is mid-journey on AWS/Azure/GCP, driving cloud engineering, platform, security, FinOps, and IaC demand.
- **Acute skills shortage** — Australia is structurally short of cyber and cloud talent. This is a *candidate-short, vacancy-rich* market, which rewards the recruiter who delivers fastest.

### Certifications worth recognising on sight

CISSP, CISM, CISA, CCSP, OSCP, CREST, SABSA, AWS/Azure/GCP architect and security certs, Kubernetes (CKA/CKAD), Terraform Associate. **CISSP** is the most common senior cyber signal; **OSCP** for offensive; **IRAP** for government assurance.

### Security clearances — a billing lever, not a footnote

For **federal government, defence, and many critical-infrastructure** roles (and System Integrators delivering to them), an Australian Government **security clearance** (Baseline, NV1, NV2, and the rare Positive Vetting) is mandatory. Clearance is granted via **AGSVA** and is sponsor-dependent. Cleared candidates command a **premium** (often $100–250+/day on contract) and are scarce — a cleared, available cyber contractor is one of the most placeable assets you can hold. Canberra is the clearance epicentre; it's an easy *adjacency* to your Sydney/Melbourne patch if you build a cleared candidate pool. Always capture clearance status and currency when qualifying.

### Who the buyers are (Sydney vs Melbourne)

- **Sydney** — financial services HQs and tech-heavy; more contract appetite. Banks/insurers, fintech, SaaS, telco, professional services, NSW government.
- **Melbourne** — strong banking (NAB, ANZ headquartered there), government (Victorian state agencies are major buyers and run structured panels), telco, utilities, and consulting. Tends to be slightly more process-driven and panel/PSA-gated for government.

## 2. The economics

Top billing is a maths problem before it's a hustle problem. Know your number and reverse-engineer the activity.

### The two revenue engines

**Permanent** is *lumpy and high-velocity per deal*: at a standard ~**18% fee** on an average cyber/cloud salary of **~$150k**, each placement bills **~$27k**. Twenty-two perm placements ≈ **$600k** in a year. Great, but each deal is one-and-done and exposed to drop-outs/counteroffers.

**Contract** is *recurring and compounding*. A contractor on a **$1,000/day** charge rate where you pay **$850/day** yields **$150/day margin = ~$750/week ≈ ~$39k annualised** per head running. The magic is the **book**: every contractor you place keeps paying margin every week until they roll off, so your run-rate accumulates.

| Contractors running | Avg margin/day | Daily GP | Annualised GP |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | $150 | $750 | ~$195k |
| 10 | $150 | $1,500 | ~$390k |
| 15 | $150 | $2,250 | ~$585k |
| 20 | $175 | $3,500 | ~$910k |

### The top-biller blend (what to actually aim for)

A genuine top biller on a 360 cyber/cloud desk typically runs **a contract book of 10–18 heads** (predictable, compounding base) **plus 1–3 perm placements a month** (spikes). That combination realistically produces **$700k–$1M+ gross profit** a year.

Your weekly split (contract M/W/F, perm T/Th) is well-suited to this: contract is urgent and call-heavy, so it gets the most days; perm is process- and relationship-heavy and runs efficiently in concentrated blocks.

### Reverse-engineering your number

Pick a 12-month GP target and back it out. Example for **$750k GP**:

- **Contract base:** build to ~12 heads running by month 6 → ~$470k run-rate.
- **Perm top-up:** ~1.5 placements/month at ~$27k → ~$280k.
- **Activity required (steady state):** ~8–12 quality contract spec calls + ~6–8 perm BD conversations a week, ~10–15 candidate CVs sent, ~6–10 candidate interviews arranged. See §3 for the full ratio model.

> **Cash maths to internalise for contract:** margin = (charge rate − pay rate) − on-costs. In Australia, watch **superannuation, payroll tax, workers' comp/insurance, and any payrolling fees** — these erode margin if you quote sloppily. Always confirm whether your charge rate is inclusive or exclusive of super and GST, and whether the contractor is PAYG or Pty Ltd/ABN.

## 3. The operating system

Billing is a *lagging* indicator. You can't control placements; you *can* control activity that produces them. Top billers obsess over **leading indicators** and protect their **prime calling time**.

### The funnel (cyber/cloud, candidate-short market)

```
Contract:  Spec/BD calls → Roles taken → CVs sent → Interviews → Offers → Placements → Extensions
Perm:      BD calls → Meetings/roles taken → CVs sent → 1st interviews → Finals → Offers → Starts
```

Rough conversion benchmarks to plan against (tune to your own data over the first quarter):

- Contract: ~1 in 4–5 quality spec calls becomes a workable role; ~3–5 CVs per interview; ~2–3 interviews per placement. Contract cycles are fast — days, not weeks.
- Perm: ~1 in 6–8 BD conversations becomes a role; ~3–4 CVs per first interview; ~2–4 first interviews per placement; cycle 3–8 weeks.

### Weekly activity targets (established consultant, steady state)

| Metric | Contract focus (M/W/F) | Perm focus (T/Th) |
|---|---|---|
| Meaningful client/BD conversations | 8–12 | 6–8 |
| New roles qualified in | 2–4 | 1–2 |
| Candidate calls (sourcing/qualifying) | 20–30 | 15–20 |
| CVs sent to clients | 8–15 | 6–10 |
| Interviews arranged | 5–8 | 3–5 |
| Candidate-led floats (MPC) | 3–5 | 2–3 |
| Contractor care / extension calls | 5–8 | — |

### The three numbers to put on your wall

1. **Spec calls / BD conversations per day** — the single most predictive number for future billings.
2. **CVs sent per week** — proves you're delivering, not just hoarding roles.
3. **Interviews arranged per week** — the closest leading indicator to revenue.

Track these daily. If billings dip in 8 weeks, it's because these dipped this week. Your HirePath **BD Research & Call Prep** and **BD Outreach** tools exist to raise the *quality* of each conversation so the ratios improve.

## 4. Business development that works

These are the methods that reliably generate roles in this market, roughly in order of ROI **for your specific situation** (warm company, cold-in-cyber consultant). Methods 1 and 2 are your priorities and get their own sections.

1. **House-account penetration** — expand into the cyber/cloud functions of accounts Novus Pathway already serves. Fastest path to a first fill. → §5
2. **Candidate-led BD (MPC marketing)** — float a hot, scarce candidate into target accounts to open doors. The most reliable door-opener in a candidate-short market. → §6
3. **Trigger-event BD** — reach out when something changes that creates hiring need:
   - New CISO / Head of Security / Head of Cloud / Head of Platform appointment (they restructure and hire).
   - A **breach or security incident** in the news (they'll be hiring weeks later).
   - **Funding round, IPO, or major contract win** (especially SIs ramping for a program).
   - **Regulatory deadline** — Essential Eight uplift, APRA/CPS, SOCI compliance programs spinning up.
   - **Cloud migration / platform modernisation** announcements.
   - **Team movement** — visible departures on LinkedIn signal a backfill.
   Set up alerts (LinkedIn, Google Alerts, news) and act within 24–48 hours.
4. **Market mapping & talent intelligence** — build a map of a target team (e.g., the security function at a target bank) using your HirePath **Market Map Builder**. Use it to (a) source and (b) BD ("I've mapped the Sydney security-architecture market — here's who's available and what they're earning").
5. **Referrals** — every candidate call ends with "who else should I speak to?" and every hiring manager you place for becomes a referrer. Placed candidates become tomorrow's clients when they move into leadership.
6. **Reactivation of lapsed clients** — Novus's dormant accounts that haven't transacted in 6–18 months. Warm-ish, low effort, often forgotten by competitors.
7. **Personal brand & content** — become the recognised voice in AU cyber/cloud recruitment so inbound comes to you. Compounds slowly; start now. → §13
8. **Events & communities** — AISA (Australian Information Security Association) chapters, BSides, OWASP, ISACA, CrowdStrike/AWS/Azure user groups and meetups in Sydney and Melbourne. Show up, don't pitch; harvest relationships.

### The non-negotiable BD principle: lead with value, not need

Cyber/cloud hiring managers are pitched daily by recruiters. The ones who get traction lead with **something useful**: a genuinely strong available candidate, a live salary/rate benchmark, a market map, or specific intel — not "do you have any roles?" Every outreach should give before it asks.

## 5. House-account penetration

**This is your single fastest route to your first cyber/cloud fill.** Novus Pathway already has trust, contracts/PSAs, and payment terms in place with these accounts. You're not selling the agency — you're extending a working relationship into a new function.

### Step 1 — Internal mapping (week one)

Sit down with the consultants who own each house account and extract:

- Which contacts they already deal with, and how warm.
- Whether there's a **PSA / preferred-supplier agreement** and what it covers (does it include contract? IT? cyber?).
- Their honest read on the account's appetite and politics.
- Permission and the best mechanism for a warm intro into the tech/security side.

Agree internally how cross-account work is split/credited *before* you start — avoid friction later.

### Step 2 — Org mapping of the cyber/cloud function

For each account, map the security and cloud org using LinkedIn + your **Market Map Builder**:

- CISO / Head of Security → their direct reports (SecOps, GRC, architecture, IAM leads).
- Head of Cloud / Platform / Infrastructure → engineering managers.
- Procurement / talent-acquisition contacts who control supplier engagement.

Identify the **economic buyer** (who owns budget) and the **hiring managers** (who feel the pain).

### Step 3 — The warm-intro approach

Best case: an existing Novus contact introduces you to the security/cloud hiring manager. Failing that, reference the relationship explicitly in outreach ("Novus has partnered with [Account] on [function] for [time] — I look after cyber & cloud and wanted to introduce myself"). Templates in §11.

### Step 4 — Land and expand

Take one small, fast, well-qualified role and **fill it brilliantly**. Speed and quality on the first req earn you the second. Then systematically widen: more managers, more functions, contract *and* perm. House accounts are where your contract **book** compounds, because the same managers re-hire and extend.

> **Target outcome:** within 6 weeks, at least one live cyber/cloud role from a house account and at least one contractor placed or in process.

## 6. Candidate-led BD

In a candidate-short market, **the candidate is the BD weapon.** When you control a genuinely scarce, immediately available profile, hiring managers take the call. This opens *new* logos that house-account work won't reach.

### How to run it

1. **Find or develop an MPC (Most Placeable Candidate).** Ideal profiles: a **cleared** cyber professional, an available **security architect**, a strong **cloud/platform engineer**, a **GRC consultant** between contracts, a well-regarded **DevOps/SRE**. Available + in-demand + credible = a door-opener.
2. **Get their permission and a tight pitch.** Two or three sentences: specialism, standout achievements, availability, rate/salary, what they want next. Anonymise enough to protect them but keep it compelling. Your HirePath **Candidate Profile Writer** turns a raw CV into this client-ready blurb.
3. **Build a target list** of 15–25 companies that would want this profile (use trigger events and the Market Map Builder).
4. **Float them** by phone first, email second (templates in §11). The ask is a *conversation*, not a hard sell: "I've got a [profile] available now — worth a quick chat even if just for your pipeline?"
5. **Convert the conversation into a role.** Even if they don't want *this* candidate, you've opened a relationship and surfaced their real needs.

### Why it works

You're not asking for a favour — you're offering scarce supply into a market that's short. It also makes you genuinely useful: you become the person who *has* the people, which is the reputation that generates inbound.

> Run **3–5 active floats a week**. One good float can open two or three new accounts.

## 7. Sourcing & delivery

Winning the role is half the job; **filling it fast** is what makes you a top biller in a candidate-short market. The recruiter with the best-prepared shortlist *first* wins.

### Sourcing channels (cyber/cloud)

- **LinkedIn Recruiter** — primary. Boolean and project pipelines.
- **Apollo** (connected in this workspace) — contact discovery and enrichment for both clients and candidates.
- **Seek Talent Search** — strong for the Australian contract market.
- **Communities** — AISA, OWASP, BSides, cloud/DevOps meetups, relevant Slack/Discord groups. Cyber talent congregates and refers.
- **GitHub** — for cloud/platform/DevSecOps engineers (look at real projects, IaC repos).
- **Referrals** — every call: "who are the two best people you've worked with?"
- **Your own redeployment pool** — contractors rolling off are your fastest fills; track end dates (see §12).

### Boolean starting points

**Cloud security engineer (Sydney):**
```
("cloud security" OR "security engineer" OR "DevSecOps") AND (AWS OR Azure OR GCP)
AND (Sydney OR "New South Wales") NOT (recruiter OR "talent acquisition")
```

**GRC / cyber risk (contract, Melbourne):**
```
("GRC" OR "security risk" OR "ISO 27001" OR "Essential Eight" OR "IRAP" OR "cyber compliance")
AND (Melbourne OR Victoria) AND (contract OR consultant OR "day rate")
```

**Platform / DevOps engineer:**
```
("platform engineer" OR "DevOps" OR "SRE" OR "site reliability")
AND (Kubernetes OR Terraform OR "CI/CD") AND (Sydney OR Melbourne)
```

Add clearance terms (`"NV1" OR "NV2" OR "Baseline" OR "security clearance" OR "AGSVA"`) when the role needs it.

### Delivery discipline

- **Screen against the brief, not the buzzwords.** Use the HirePath **CV Screener** to rank a longlist against the JD and produce a scored shortlist fast.
- **Send a tight shortlist (3–4), not a CV dump.** Quality signals expertise and protects your reputation with the hiring manager.
- **Write the profile, don't forward the CV.** Use the **Candidate Profile Writer** to present a client-ready summary (fit, motivations, availability, rate).
- **Prep every candidate** with the **Interview Prep Pack** — this is the single biggest lever on offer rates. A well-prepped candidate converts dramatically better.
- **Control the process** — book interviews tight, debrief both sides within an hour, manage expectations on rate/salary and notice/availability from day one.

## 8. Qualifying

Most wasted effort comes from working bad roles and unqualified candidates. Qualify hard. Saying no to a bad role is a billing decision.

### Qualifying a role (before you work it)

- **Exact requirement** — must-haves vs nice-to-haves, tech stack, clearance, seniority.
- **Why open / why now** — growth, backfill, project? (Tells you urgency and stability.)
- **Rate/salary & budget** — get a real number, confirm it's market-realistic. Push back early if it isn't.
- **Process** — who interviews, how many stages, timeline, decision-maker.
- **Competition** — exclusive or are five agencies on it? How many CVs already in?
- **Commercials** — is there a PSA? Fee/margin agreed? Terms signed?
- **Contract specifics** — duration, extension likelihood, PAYG vs ABN, inside/outside, charge-rate ceiling, when timesheets/payment run.

If a role is contended, capped on rate, and slow to decide, deprioritise it. Work the exclusive, urgent, realistic ones.

### Qualifying a candidate

- **Motivation** — *why* are they moving? (Money-only movers are counteroffer risks.)
- **Availability / notice** — contractors: when free, current end date. Perm: notice period.
- **Rate/salary expectation** — real number, and the floor they'd accept.
- **Process status** — what else are they in? Where? Manage against your timeline.
- **Clearance** — level and currency (critical for gov/defence).
- **Logistics** — location, on-site/hybrid/remote expectations, work rights/visa.
- **Counteroffer & close** — pre-close from the first conversation; surface counteroffer risk early.

> A pre-closed, fully-qualified candidate sent to an exclusive, well-qualified role is how top billers make placements look easy.

## 9. The weekly plan

Your structure — **contract Monday/Wednesday/Friday, perm Tuesday/Thursday** — is sound. Contract is urgent and high-frequency, so it gets three days; perm is process-heavy and runs well in two focused blocks. The key is that **a "focus day" is about where your BD and new-role energy goes, not a hard wall** — you still service live perm interviews on a Wednesday and answer a contractor on a Tuesday. Focus controls *proactive* effort; reactive delivery happens whenever it's needed.

### Anchor principles

- **Protect prime calling time.** ~9:30–11:30 and ~2:00–4:00 are when clients and candidates answer. No admin, no LinkedIn rabbit-holes — calls only. Block it in your calendar as recurring busy time.
- **Front-load BD.** Do the hardest, highest-value proactive work (BD calls, floats) early before the day fills with reactive noise.
- **Theme the days but stay responsive.** Live interviews, offers, and contractor issues get handled the day they happen regardless of theme.
- **End every day with tomorrow's plan** — names, numbers, and the first three calls written down before you leave.

### Weekly skeleton

| Day | Theme | Primary proactive focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | Contract | Plan the contract week; chase live processes from last week; spec calls into house accounts; redeployment of contractors rolling off; new contract BD. |
| **Tuesday** | Perm | Perm BD into house accounts & target list; candidate-led floats; perm interview prep & process management; deeper candidate generation. |
| **Wednesday** | Contract | Heaviest contract BD & spec-call day; contractor care/extension calls; fast shortlisting and CV sends. |
| **Thursday** | Perm | Perm finals, offers and closes; new perm BD; market mapping; content. |
| **Friday** | Contract | Close out contract placements & paperwork; extensions confirmed; weekend availability checks; pipeline build for Monday; week review & numbers. |

### Weekly rhythm rituals

- **Monday AM (30 min):** review the funnel numbers from last week; set this week's targets (calls, CVs, interviews); identify the three deals most likely to close.
- **Wednesday midday checkpoint:** are you on pace for the week's CV-sent and interview-arranged targets? Course-correct.
- **Friday PM (45 min):** update the CRM fully; confirm next week's interviews; log billings & pipeline; write Monday's first three calls. **Confirm every contractor's timesheet is in.**

## 12. Contract desk specifics

Contract is where your **compounding book** lives — and where small operational mistakes cost real margin. Run it tightly.

### Protect and grow the book

- **Track every contractor's end date** in the CRM with reminders at **6, 4, and 2 weeks out.** Extensions are the cheapest billings you'll ever get — never let one lapse by surprise.
- **Care calls** keep contractors loyal and surface extension/expansion signals early ("we're bringing on two more next quarter"). A happy contractor is also a referral engine.
- **Redeploy proactively.** A contractor rolling off is your fastest next placement — start floating them 4 weeks before they finish.

### Margin hygiene

- Confirm whether the **charge rate is inclusive/exclusive of GST and super**, and **PAYG vs ABN/Pty Ltd**.
- Account for **on-costs** (super, payroll tax, workers' comp, insurances, payrolling fees) before quoting a margin — quote the *net* margin, not the gross spread.
- Hold your margin: in a candidate-short market, scarce skills justify it. Don't reflexively discount.

### Compliance (Australia)

Get this right or placements fall over and your agency carries risk:

- **Right to work / visa** checks.
- **Contracts signed** by client and contractor *before* a start date.
- **Insurances** where the contractor operates via an ABN/Pty Ltd (professional indemnity, public liability).
- **Timesheets** — set the expectation on day one; chase them every Friday. No timesheet = no invoice = no margin that week.
- **Police checks / clearances** verified and current for gov/defence work.

## 13. Personal brand & content

Inbound is the highest-margin BD there is. In cyber/cloud, the recruiter known as the genuine specialist gets the call before the role hits the market. This compounds slowly — start in week one and keep going.

### Content pillars (rotate)

1. **Market & rate intelligence** — "What cleared GRC consultants are charging in Sydney right now." Original data beats opinion every time.

- **Comment daily** on posts from target hiring managers and candidates — visibility without pitching.

> Build the salary guide as a by-product of your normal qualifying calls: every candidate and client conversation already surfaces a rate. Capture it. By quarter-end you'll have proprietary data nobody else has.

## 15. The 90-day plan

This is the plan for your exact situation: experienced consultant, **cold in cyber/cloud**, at an agency with **warm house accounts**. The goal of 90 days is a **live pipeline, a first contract placement, and the foundations of a compounding book.**

### Phase 1 — Learn & map (Weeks 1–4)

**Outcome: market fluency, a mapped opportunity, and live candidate floats.**

- **Week 1 — Immersion.** Learn the role taxonomy, rates, certs, and clearances in §1. Sit with each house-account owner; map contacts, PSAs, and warm-intro routes (§5). Find out which government panels Novus can transact through. Set up CRM, LinkedIn Recruiter, Apollo, and alerts.
- **Week 2 — Map the functions.** For the top 3–5 house accounts, map the cyber/cloud orgs (Market Map Builder). Identify hiring managers and economic buyers. Build a target-list of 15–25 *new* logos by trigger event. Start building a candidate pool — call 25–30 cyber/cloud professionals to learn the market and find your first MPCs.
- **Week 3 — First floats & warm intros.** Secure 2–3 strong available candidates with permission to float. Begin warm-intro outreach into house accounts (templates A, B). Start candidate-led floats into the target list (§6). Post your first LinkedIn content.
- **Week 4 — Convert to conversations.** Book client calls/meetings off the floats and warm intros. Aim for your **first qualified cyber/cloud role.** Keep the candidate pool growing and capturing rate data.

### Phase 2 — Land & fill (Weeks 5–8)

**Outcome: first placement(s) and a working pipeline.**

- Work your first roles with total focus — exclusive, well-qualified ones first. Deliver tight shortlists fast (CV Screener → Profile Writer), prep every candidate (Interview Prep Pack).
- Land your **first contract placement** — speed matters most here; contract cycles are days. Then immediately ask that manager for the next role and a referral.
- Keep BD constant: 8–10 quality conversations/week, 3–5 floats/week. Don't let delivery starve your pipeline (the classic boom-bust trap).
- Begin tracking contractor end dates the moment anyone starts. Publish your first mini rate-insight post from the data you're gathering.

### Phase 3 — Build the book (Weeks 9–12)

**Outcome: a compounding base and repeatable BD.**

- Convert house-account wins into repeat business — more managers, more functions, perm *and* contract.
- Grow the contract book toward 4–6 heads running; manage first extensions (your easiest billings).
- Turn placed candidates and hiring managers into referrers; reactivate 5–10 lapsed accounts.
- Lock in the rhythm: themed days, protected prime-call time, Friday numbers review. Publish your first quarterly **Sydney/Melbourne cyber & cloud rate guide** — your BD opener for the next year.

**By day 90 you should have:** market fluency; 2–5 placements made or in final stages; a contract book starting to compound; a live multi-account pipeline; a candidate pool with proprietary rate data; and a repeatable weekly operating system.

## 16. Pitfalls

- **Faking domain knowledge.** Cyber/cloud people spot it instantly. Learn the language (§1); when you don't know, ask — curiosity reads better than bluffing.
- **Boom-bust (delivery starving BD).** When you get busy filling, BD stops, and 8 weeks later the pipeline is empty. **Protect BD time even in your busiest week.**
- **Working bad roles.** Contended, rate-capped, slow-decision roles burn time for no placement. Qualify hard; say no (§8).
- **CV dumping.** Sending six average CVs instead of three excellent ones erodes trust. Quality shortlists signal expertise.
- **Letting extensions lapse.** The cheapest billings you'll ever get, lost to a missed calendar reminder. Track every end date.
- **Margin leakage.** Quoting gross spread without on-costs, or reflexively discounting scarce skills. Know your true net margin and hold it.
- **Skipping interview prep.** The single biggest avoidable cause of lost placements. Prep every candidate, every time.
- **Single-touch BD.** One unanswered email and you give up. Run the multi-touch, multi-channel cadence (§11K).
- **Neglecting candidate care.** Today's contractor is tomorrow's hiring manager and three referrals. Stay close.
- **Compliance shortcuts.** An unsigned contract or missing right-to-work check can collapse a placement and expose the agency. Never skip it.
